# Abstract

<div style="text-align: justify;">
Safety hysteresis, a dynamic pattern reflecting how traffic safety deteriorates and recovers during disturbances, remains largely unexplored. Using real-world trajectory datasets, this study demonstrates that safety hysteresis exists at both the vehicle-pair and platoon levels, manifested as counterclockwise loops on spacing-TTC<sup>−1</sup> diagrams and clockwise loops on density-TTC<sup>−1</sup> diagrams. To quantify these patterns, we propose the Safety Hysteresis Intensity (HI), defined as the normalized area enclosed by the safety hysteresis loop. Empirical analyses show that naturalistic traffic exhibits substantially higher safety HI than controlled experiments, while ACC and autonomous vehicles reduce safety HI and promote safer conditions. Correlation analyses reveal that low-speed regimes, large inter-vehicle speed differences, and abrupt acceleration or deceleration amplify pair-level safety HI, whereas variability in flow, density, and average speed governs platoon-level safety HI. Complementary simulations further clarify these causal mechanisms, showing that higher braking intensity and greater macroscopic fluctuations lead to stronger safety hysteresis. The results highlight safety HI as a system-level diagnostic indicator of safety. By incorporating hysteresis dynamics into safety evaluation, this study offers new theoretical perspectives and practical insights for improving traffic safety.
</div>

# Quantification of Safety Hysteresis

- Pair-level safety Hysteresis Intensity (HI):

$$
I_s = \frac{|A|}{\Delta s_A}
$$

**where:**

- \|A\| — area of the hysteresis loop A on the spacing-TTC<sup>−1</sup> diagram for vehicle pairs
- Δs<sub>A</sub> — corresponding spacing span

- Platoon-level safety HI:

$$
\mathbb{I}_s = \frac{|\mathbb{A}|}{\Delta k_\mathbb{A}}
$$

**where:**

- \|𝔸\| — area of the hysteresis loop 𝔸
- Δk<sub>𝔸</sub> — corresponding density span

# Simulation Experiments

<div style="text-align: justify;">
Controlled IDM simulations isolate the effect of each factor, complementing the empirical correlations.
</div>

- **Vehicle-pair level.** Stronger braking and lower post-deceleration speeds raise safety HI, while acceleration rate and initial speed have little independent effect.

  ![9](https://jerry-zpl.github.io/images/2026-TRC-9.png)

- **Platoon level.** Greater variability (standard deviation and range) in density, flow, and average speed consistently amplifies safety HI.

  ![10](https://jerry-zpl.github.io/images/2026-TRC-10.png)

**Takeaway:** smoothing deceleration and stabilizing macroscopic flow are the two most effective levers for reducing safety hysteresis.

# Relation to Traditional Traffic Hysteresis

Does safety hysteresis simply inherit the direction of traditional traffic hysteresis? It does not — the safety loop keeps a fixed orientation regardless of the traditional loop's direction.

<div style="text-align: justify;">
- **Vehicle-pair level.** Both timid (CW) and aggressive (CCW) traditional loops yield a counterclockwise safety loop: a physical necessity, since a loop can only begin when the follower is faster than the leader, and that same condition forces the counterclockwise direction.
</div>

  ![11](https://jerry-zpl.github.io/images/2026-TRC-11.png)

<div style="text-align: justify;">
- **Platoon level.** Likewise, both clockwise and counterclockwise traditional loops predominantly map to positive (clockwise) safety HI, though aggressive driving raises the chance of the opposite sign.
</div>

  ![12](https://jerry-zpl.github.io/images/2026-TRC-12.png)

# Conclusion

<div style="text-align: justify;">
- This study provides the first comprehensive examination of safety hysteresis using large-scale trajectory data, showing it exists at both the vehicle-pair and platoon levels, and proposes Safety Hysteresis Intensity (HI), the loop area normalized by its span.
</div>

<div style="text-align: justify;">
- Naturalistic datasets (NGSIM, Waymo) exhibit higher HI than controlled ACC experiments; both ACC activation and autonomous driving significantly reduce safety HI.
</div>

<div style="text-align: justify;">
- Low-speed regimes, large leader–follower speed differences, and abrupt acceleration or deceleration are key contributors to pair-level HI, while larger fluctuations in flow, density, and speed govern platoon-level HI; ACC penetration mitigates these effects.
</div>

<div style="text-align: justify;">
- IDM simulations clarify these mechanisms, indicating that safety hysteresis can be reduced by smoothing deceleration profiles, damping traffic oscillations, and leveraging ACC/AV technologies.
</div>
