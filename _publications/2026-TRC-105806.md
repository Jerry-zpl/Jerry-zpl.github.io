# Abstract

<div style="text-align: justify;">
Safety hysteresis, a dynamic pattern reflecting how traffic safety deteriorates and recovers during disturbances, remains largely unexplored. Using real-world trajectory datasets, this study demonstrates that safety hysteresis exists at both the vehicle-pair and platoon levels, manifested as counterclockwise loops on spacing-TTC$$^{-1}$$ diagrams and clockwise loops on density-TTC$$^{-1}$$ diagrams. To quantify these patterns, we propose the Safety Hysteresis Intensity (HI), defined as the normalized area enclosed by the safety hysteresis loop. Empirical analyses show that naturalistic traffic exhibits substantially higher safety HI than controlled experiments, while ACC and autonomous vehicles reduce safety HI and promote safer conditions. Correlation analyses reveal that low-speed regimes, large inter-vehicle speed differences, and abrupt acceleration or deceleration amplify pair-level safety HI, whereas variability in flow, density, and average speed governs platoon-level safety HI. Complementary simulations further clarify these causal mechanisms, showing that higher braking intensity and greater macroscopic fluctuations lead to stronger safety hysteresis. The results highlight safety HI as a system-level diagnostic indicator of safety. By incorporating hysteresis dynamics into safety evaluation, this study offers new theoretical perspectives and practical insights for improving traffic safety.
</div>

# Quantification of Safety Hysteresis -- Safety Hysteresis Intensity (HI)

- Pair level:

$$
\begin{equation}
	I_s = \frac{|A|}{\Delta s_A}
\end{equation}
$$

 **Where:**  
  - $|A|$: area of the hysteresis loop $A$ on the spacing-TTC$^{-1}$ diagram for vehicle pairs
  - $\Delta s_A$: corresponding spacing span

- Platoon level:

$$
\begin{equation}
	\mathbb{I}_s = \frac{|\mathbb{A}|}{\Delta k_\mathbb{A}}
\end{equation}
$$

 **Where:**  
  -  $|\mathbb{A}|$: area of the hysteresis loop $\mathbb{A}$
  - $\Delta k_\mathbb{A}$ represents its density span
